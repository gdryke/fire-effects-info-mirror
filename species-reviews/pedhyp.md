## Introduction

## Taxonomy

The currently accepted scientific name of subterranean Indian breadroot is _Pediomelum hypogaeum_ (Nutt.) Rydb. (Fabaceae). Recognized varieties are [20]:

 * _Pediomelum hypogaeum_ (Nutt.) Rydb. var. _hypogaea_
 * _Pediomelum hypogaeum_ (Nutt.) Rydb. var. _scaposum_ (Gray) Mahler
 * _Pediomelum hypogaeum_ (Nutt.) Rydb. var. _subulatum_ (Bush) Grimes

 _Pediomelum hypogaeum_ var. _scaposum_ occurs in east-central Texas, and _Pediomelum hypogaeum_ var. _subulatum_ occurs in eastern Texas. Varietal differences are based upon peduncle length and degree of pubescence [12].

### Synonyms

 * _Psoralea hypogaea_ Nutt. [1,5,6,9,16]
 * _Psoralea hypogaea_ var. _hypogaea_
 * _Psoralea hypogaea_ var. _scaposa_ Gray
 * _Psoralea hypogaea_ var. _subulata_ (Bush) Grimes [9,12]

### Other Common Names

little breadroot, little breadroot scurf-pea, prairie turnip, Indian turnip, prairie potato, subterranean breadroot

## Distribution

## General Distribution

Subterranean Indian breadroot is distributed from eastern Montana and eastern Wyoming south through eastern Colorado, western Kansas, and western Oklahoma to northeastern New Mexico, Texas, and extreme southwestern Arkansas [6,9,12].

### States and Provinces

 * United States: AR CO KS MT NE NM OK TX WY

## Site Characteristics

Subterranean Indian breadroot grows in rocky or sandy soils. It is found on bluffs, plains, and stream valleys [9].

## Plant Communities

Subterranean Indian breadroot occurs in plains grasslands and prairies of the Great Plains. See the Appendix for plant communities and cover types in which subterranean Indian breadroot may occur.

## Botanical and Ecological Characteristics

## Botanical Description

Subterranean Indian breadroot is a native perennial forb. It is acaulescent or rarely short-stemmed. The taproot is a thick, woody storage organ located 1 to 3 inches (3-8 cm) below ground. The caudex is also usually located below ground. The fruit is a legume [1,9,16].

### Raunkiaer Life Form (Raunkiaer 1934)

 * Geophyte

## Seasonal Development

Subterranean Indian breadroot flowers from May to June [9].

## Regeneration Processes

Subterranean Indian breadroot reproduces from seed [9]. Seeds of Psoralea species require scarification before germination [14].

## Fire Ecology and Management

## Immediate Fire Effects

Fire probably top-kills subterranean Indian breadroot.

## Postfire Regeneration Strategy (Stickney 1989)

 * Caudex, growing points in soil
 * Ground residual colonizer (on-site, initial community)

## Fire Adaptations

Fire-related information on subterranean Indian breadroot is lacking in the literature. Subterranean Indian breadroot probably survives fire because the caudex is located below ground. Presumably, starch reserves in the root are metabolized the growing season following fire, and plants sprout from the caudex. Fire probably helps stimulate germination of seedbank-stored subterranean Indian breadroot seeds by scarifying them.

## Nonfire Management Considerations

## Federal Status

None

## Other Status

The Natural Heritage Program lists Pediomelum hypogaeum var. hypogaea as threatened in Montana [19].

## Importance to Wildlife and Livestock

The foliage of Pediomelum species are suspected of being poisonous [3,17]. Their seeds contain skin-photosensitizing chemicals [10].

### Palatability and Nutritional Value

Pediomelum species are unpalatable to domestic sheep and cattle [17].

Subterranean Indian breadroot taproots contain 69.8 percent starch and 7 percent protein (dry weight), and are high in vitamin C (17.1 mg/100 g dry weight) [4,14].
