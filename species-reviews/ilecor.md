# Ilex coriacea — large gallberry

**Written:** August, 1991
  
**Contributors:** Milo J. Coladonato - 1st Author

## Citation

## Introduction

## Taxonomy

The currently accepted scientific name for large gallberry is _Ilex coriacea_ (Pursh) Chapm. (Aquifoliaceae). The _Ilex_ genus consists of 13 species [9,15].

### Synonyms

 * _Prinos lucidus_ Ait.
 * _Prinos coriaceus_ Pursh
 * _Ilex lucida_ (Ait) Torr. & Gray ex Wats.

### Other Common Names

sweet gallberry, bay-gallbush

## Distribution

## General Distribution

Large gallberry grows along the Gulf and Southeastern coastal plains of the United States. Its range extends from southeastern Virginia south to northern and central Florida, and west to eastern Texas [2,3]. Large gallberry extends inland from the Atlantic coast to eastern Tennessee and from the Gulf coast to Arkansas [11].

### States and Provinces

 * United States: AL AR FL GA KY LA MS NC OK SC TN TX VA

## Site Characteristics

Large gallberry grows in pine savannas and flatwoods, shrub-tree bogs and bays, open bogs, seepage areas in woodlands, on lower slopes, wooded ravines, and depressions [7,11].

Typical soils are sandy, acidic, and low in organic matter. They are often poorly drained, and frequently or seasonally flooded [2,7].

Large gallberry is common on longleaf pine or longleaf pine-slash pine sites. Overstory associates include baldcypress and pondcypress (Taxodium spp.), blackgum (Nyssa sylvatica), and water tupelo (N. aquatica) [8,11].

Understory associates include saw-palmetto (Serenoa repens), sweetbay (Magnolia virginiana), swamp cyrilla (Cyrilla racemiflora), bitter gallberry (Ilex galbra), laurelleaf greenbrier (Smilax laurifolia), buckwheat-tree (Cliftonia monophyla), blueberries (Vaccinium spp.), and blackberries (Rubus spp.) [8,25].

## Plant Communities

Large gallberry is a dominant understory species in the longleaf-slash pine forest community [24].

## Botanical and Ecological Characteristics

## Botanical Description

Large gallberry is a small evergreen tree or large shrub, native to the southeastern United States. Its fruits are shiny black berries. Plants grow up to 7 feet (2.1 m) tall. The leaves are leathery. The flowers are usually unisexual; the plants are dioecious [7,9,11].

### Raunkiaer Life Form (Raunkiaer 1934)

 * Phanerophyte
 * Cryptophyte

## Seasonal Development

Large gallberry flowers between April and May; its fruit ripens from September to October [7,11,27].

## Regeneration Processes

Large gallberry regenerates vegetatively by sprouting from rhizomes [5]. It also regenerates sexually, although the details have not been studied [27]. The nature of the fruit suggests that the seeds are dispersed by animals.

## Successional Status

Large gallberry is a mid to late seral species that grows well under a canopy. It tends toward a mature forest and competes with tree seedlings [6,19]. It typically does not on sites lacking the shade of a dense canopy [17,18].

## Fire Ecology and Management

## Immediate Fire Effects

Large gallberry has very flammable foliage. Most fires top-kill the plant [18].

## Postfire Regeneration Strategy (Stickney 1989)

 * survivor species; on site surviving rhizomes
 * off site colonizer; seed carried by animals or water; post fire yr 1&2

## Fire Adaptations

Large gallberry is sensitive to fire, but survives by resprouting from rhizomes [5,29].

## Plant Response to Fire

Large gallberry sprouts from underground rhizomes after fire [29].

## Fire Management Considerations

Prescribed burning on a 1- to 3-year cycle effectively controls large gallberry [29].

## Nonfire Management Considerations

## Federal Status

None

## Other Status

No entry.

## Importance to Wildlife and Livestock

Large gallberry is not a preferred livestock forage, but livestock will eat branches of young plants when other forage is scarce [14,23].

### Palatability and Nutritional Value

The palatability of large gallberry to white-tailed deer is considered fair, although deer feed on more preferable forage when available [4,14].

### Cover Value

Large gallberry provides security cover for a variety of reptile and amphibian species in the southern coastal parts of its range [21].

## Value for Rehabilitation or Restoration of Disturbed Sites

Large gallberry presumably provides watershed protection and erosion control.

## Other Uses

No entry.

## Other Management Considerations

Pine growth is reduced by overtopping of seedlings or proximity to large gallberry and other shrubs [28]. Large gallberry can be controlled by streamline basal application of the herbicide Garlon 4 [19].
