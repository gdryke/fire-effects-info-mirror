## Introduction

## Taxonomy

The scientific name of elk sedge is _Carex garberi_ Fern. (Cyperaceae) [7,8,15].

### Synonyms

 * _Carex garberi_ subsp._garberi_
 * _Carex garberi_ subsp._bifaria_(Fern.) Hulten [1,7]

### Other Common Names

Garber sedge

## Distribution

## General Distribution

Elk sedge occurs from Quebec south to New York and west to Indiana. From Quebec it extends west to British Columbia and north to Alaska. In the western cordillera elk sedge extends from British Columbia east to Alberta and south to California, Nevada, and Idaho [1,4,6].

### States and Provinces

 * United States: AK CA ID IN ME MI NV NY ND OH OR PA UT WA
 * Canada: AB BC MB NB ON PQ SK YT

## Site Characteristics

Elk sedge is found in swamps, on the margins of ponds [6], and in wet places [7]. It is found on calcareous sands, gravels, and ledges, especially near the Great Lakes [4,10,15]. In Michigan it occurs on wet sandy, gravelly, or marly shores, interdunal flats, rock crevices, and at the edges of northern white-cedar (_Thuja occidentalis_) thickets [15]. In Maine it occurs on riverbanks [12].

## Botanical and Ecological Characteristics

## Botanical Description

Elk sedge is a native, perennial, monoecious graminoid. It is loosely caespitose. Culms are 19.7 to 27.6 inches (0.5-0.7 m) tall [1], firm, and triangular [6]. Leaves are shorter to much taller than the culms [4], and 0.8 to 0.16 inches (2-4 mm) wide. The terminal inflorescence is 0.24 to 0.79 inches (6 to 20 mm) long [1]. The achene is 0.06 inches (1.5 mm) long. The perigynia surrounding the achene is lenticular and 0.8 to 0.12 inches (2-3 mm) long [1]. Elk sedge is stoloniferous or rhizomatous, the rhizome being elongated [1,6].

## Seasonal Development

Elk sedge blooms from June to August, depending on location [4,10,12].

## Regeneration Processes

Elk sedge sprouts from perennating buds at the base of the culms [6] and it reproduces vegetatively by rhizomes [1]. It also reproduces by seed [6].

## Fire Ecology and Management

## Immediate Fire Effects

Elk sedge culms are probably killed by fire during the growing season.

## Postfire Regeneration Strategy (Stickney 1989)

 * Rhizomatous herb, rhizome in soil
 * Tussock graminoid

## Fire Adaptations

Although culms are probably killed by fire during the growing season, elk sedge probably sprouts from rhizomes [6] after the aerial portions are burned.
