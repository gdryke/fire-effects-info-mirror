## Introduction

## Taxonomy

The currently accepted scientific name for pink azalea is _Rhododendron periclymenoides_ (Michx.) Schinners [9,10,17]. There are no recognized subspecies, varieties, or forms.

### Synonyms

 * _Rhododendron nudiflora_(L.) Torr _. Azalea nudiflora L._

### Other Common Names

wild-honeysuckle, purple-honeysuckle, pinxterbloom azalea, pinxter flower, election-pink

## Distribution

## General Distribution

Pink azalea grows mostly in the mountainous regions in the eastern United States from southeastern New York south through the Appalachian Mountains to Georgia. Outlying populations occur in southern Indiana, Ohio, and Illinois [1,9,17].

### [States](<https://research.fs.usda.gov/feis/statesprovinceskey>)

 * United States: AL CT DE GA IL IN IA KY MD MA NJ NY NC OH PA SC TN VA WV

## Site Characteristics

Pink azalea commonly occurs in mixed deciduous forests along stream bottoms, bogs, shaded mountain sides, and ravines [1]. Wild honeysuckle flourishes on well-drained acid soils in cool, moist locations [2,9,17].

## Plant Communities

Pink azalea commonly occurs in mixed deciduous forests [1]. Some common tree associates of pink azalea are eastern hemlock (_Tsuga canadensis_), pitch pine (_Pinus rigida_), blackgum (_Nyssa sylvatica_), ironwood (_Ostrya virginiana_), oak (_Quercus_ spp.), and birch (_Betula_ spp.). Some common understory associates include rosebay rhododendron (_Rhododendron maximum_), flowering dogwood (_Cornus florida_), mountain laurel (_Kalmia latifolia_), highbush blueberry (_Vaccinium corymbosum_), and maple-leaved viburnum (_Viburnum acerifolium_) [7,8,15].

## Botanical and Ecological Characteristics

## Botanical Description

Pink azalea is a low, erect, deciduous, stoloniferous shrub which grows to 6.0 feet (2.0 m) in height. It has open branches, sparse foliage, and forms dense thickets. The leaves are simple, alternate, and mostly in clusters at the tips of the branches. The tubular, individual flowers are borne in small terminal clusters. The fruit is a many-seeded, woody capsule [1,2,20].

### Raunkiaer Life Form

 * Phanerophyte [18]

## Seasonal Development

Pink azalea flowering dates are from March to May. The fruit ripens late in the summer and the seed is dispersed in the late fall [16].

## Regeneration Processes

Pink azalea can reproduce by seed, although details have not been described. It can also regenerate by layering, sprouting from the root crown, or by sending out horizontal stems that root at the nodes [3,9,20]. Pink azalea seed are probably dispersed by small birds and mammals.

## Successional Status

Pink azalea is very shade tolerant [12]. Lipscombe [13] reports that pink azalea produces a majority of its growth after canopy closure.

## Fire Ecology and Management

## Immediate Fire Effects

Fire typically top-kills pink azalea [4,22].

## Postfire Regeneration Strategy

 * Small shrub, adventitious-bud root crown
 * Secondary colonizer - off-site seed [23]

## Fire Adaptations

Pink azalea typically survives fire by sprouting from the root crown. Birds and small mammals may transport some seed to burned sites [4,22]

## Plant Response to Fire

In New England, pink azalea recovers quickly after light fires but more slowly after moderate to severe fires [22].

Pink azalea provides cover for a variety of birds and mammals [12,16].

## Value for Rehabilitation or Restoration of Disturbed Sites

Pink azalea affords erosion control and steep watershed protection in the mountains of the eastern United States [20].

## Other Uses

Pink azalea has been cultivated as an ornamental [16].
